# The Sample Voyage

## Part One: Leaving Home

The town slept under a gray sky when Anne closed the door behind her. She
carried one small bag, a letter she had never sent, and the stubborn feeling
that the sea owed her an answer.

Her brother had begged her to stay until spring. The roads were bad, he said,
and the ships worse; but spring was a promise nobody kept, and she had
stopped waiting for promises.

At the harbour office a clerk read her ticket twice, frowned twice, and
stamped it anyway. Freedom, she thought, often looks like a tired man with a
rubber stamp.

## Part Two: The Crossing

The first days at sea were kind. The wind pushed them gently west, and the
passengers learned each other's names, habits, and small lies.

On the fifth night a storm found them. It shook the ship like a dog shakes a
rag, and it taught everyone aboard how loudly they could pray.

When the water calmed, the captain gathered them on deck and counted heads.
Nobody was missing, though several dinners were.

Anne wrote in her notebook: the sea does not hate us; it simply does not
remember that we are here. She underlined nothing, because everything seemed
equally true.

The last morning smelled of coal and oranges. A low coastline rose slowly
out of the haze, and the passengers fell silent, each one privately
rehearsing a new life.
